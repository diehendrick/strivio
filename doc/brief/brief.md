1. Executive Summary
This project concerns the development of a mobile application that combines personalized nutrition planning, adaptive workout scheduling, and digital coaching within a single, unified platform. The product addresses a clear gap in the market: most existing fitness apps focus on either nutrition or training, while professional personal coaching remains expensive and time-consuming for the average user.
The application will deliver an adaptive, data-driven experience where schedules evolve alongside the user's progress, supported by integrated digital guidance. The brief covers product vision, target audience, feature scope, structural architecture, design direction, and the commercial model.

2. Project Goal
To design and develop an application that integrates nutrition, workouts, and digital guidance into a single personalized and adaptive system, providing users with a credible, affordable alternative to traditional personal training.
2.1 Problem Statement

Many users lack the knowledge to properly structure nutrition and training.
Existing applications typically address only one component (nutrition or workouts).
Personal coaching is expensive and time-intensive, creating accessibility barriers.
Available nutrition apps that include workouts often default to short or generic exercises (e.g., home workouts) rather than complete, structured training programs.

2.2 Solution Statement
A digital platform offering personalized and adaptive schedules built from user data and goals — schedules that grow with the user, save time and money, and centralize all relevant tracking in one location.

3. Product Vision
To offer users a single central platform for achieving their sports and nutrition goals, where all relevant data is clearly tracked, and where structured personalization replaces fragmented self-management.
The application brings nutrition, workouts, and guidance together in one central system and actively supports the user in following a personalized plan — mirroring the experience offered by an in-person gym coach.

5. User Flow (High-Level)

Login / Account Access
Intake Form — User submits personal information, goals, and preferences.
Coach Selection — User chooses a personal (digital) coach.
Plan Generation — The app automatically generates:
A nutrition plan with daily caloric and nutritional targets.
A workout plan scheduled across the upcoming two weeks, based on user-selected training frequency and adjustable by specific days.


Workout Execution — Workouts are performed in-app with explanations, images, and video guidance.
Progress Tracking — Daily personal log; training data is recorded automatically, nutrition logged manually.
Ongoing Adaptation — Schedules evolve with the user's data and progress.

Review Notes
The following section documents the weaknesses, gaps, and assumptions identified during the construction of this brief. These should be addressed before the document is treated as a finalized reference.
Weaknesses

No defined success metrics or KPIs. The source material does not specify how the success of the product will be measured, making it impossible to validate outcomes post-launch.
No timeline or milestones. The brief lacks scheduling information, project phases, deadlines, or release targets.
No stakeholder mapping. Roles, decision-makers, and approvers are not identified in the source material.
Pricing and subscription tiers undefined. The freemium model is mentioned, but the boundary between free and paid features is not precisely delineated, and pricing is absent.
Platform ambiguity. The source does not state whether the app is intended for iOS, Android, web, or all of these.
Coach definition is ambiguous. It is unclear whether the "personal coach" is a human, an AI persona, a hybrid, or a UI metaphor — this affects scope, cost, and feature complexity significantly.
No technical constraints or stack guidance. No mention of backend, AI/ML provider, integrations (e.g., Apple Health, Google Fit, wearables), or data privacy/regulatory requirements (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA).
No accessibility requirements. No reference to WCAG compliance, language localization, or inclusive design standards.
No competitor specifics. Although competitor research is cited as the basis for navigation and color choices, no competitors are named, limiting traceability.
Out-of-scope items not declared. The absence of explicit exclusions risks scope creep during design and development.

Assumptions Made

The app is mobile-first / mobile-only, inferred from references to "screens," "tabs," and intake-led flows.
The "personal coach" feature refers to a digital/AI coach, supported by the chat feature and freemium positioning (a human coach would not scale within a freemium tier).
The two-week workout cycle is a recurring/regenerating window rather than a one-time schedule.
The current white + dark blue color direction is preliminary and may be evolved during the visual design phase.
The brief is intended for an internal design and product team rather than external clients or investors.
Source images represent the complete current scope; no additional unreferenced features are implied.
The four-page architecture (Home, Logbook, Coach, More) is stable and not subject to restructuring at this stage, though logical refinement is permitted.
